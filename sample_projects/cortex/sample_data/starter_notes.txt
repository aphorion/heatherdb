# Systems & Failures
Stock market crashes often cascade: one large sell triggers margin calls, which trigger more sells, creating a self-reinforcing spiral.
Distributed systems fail when correlated failures overwhelm redundancy — a single bug can take down every replica simultaneously.
Bank runs are self-fulfilling prophecies: the fear of collapse causes the collapse.
Circuit breakers in financial markets halt trading when prices drop too fast, preventing cascade failures.
Load balancers distribute traffic to prevent any single server from becoming overwhelmed and failing.
A single point of failure can bring down an entire system, no matter how robust the other components are.

# Nature & Ecology
Forest fires spread through connected dry brush — firebreaks work by removing the connections, not by fighting the fire itself.
Predator-prey cycles oscillate because success creates the conditions for failure: too many predators starve, prey rebounds, predators return.
Coral reefs are the most interconnected ecosystems on Earth — when one species declines, cascading effects ripple through hundreds of others.
Mycorrhizal networks connect trees underground, allowing them to share nutrients and warning signals about threats.
Ant colonies solve complex optimization problems through simple local rules — no individual ant understands the global plan.
Keystone species have disproportionate influence: remove one and the entire ecosystem restructures.

# Biology & Medicine
Epidemics spread through networks of human contact — reducing connections (social distancing) slows transmission exponentially.
Vaccines work by pre-training the immune system: a weakened version of the threat creates memory without the danger.
Hebbian learning: neurons that fire together wire together, strengthening connections through repeated co-activation.
The immune system remembers every pathogen it has ever fought — a biological one-shot learning system.
Antibiotic resistance spreads through horizontal gene transfer: bacteria share survival strategies across species boundaries.
Sleep consolidates memories by replaying the day's experiences, strengthening important connections and pruning weak ones.

# Society
Rumors spread like epidemics: each person who believes becomes a new transmission vector.
Traffic jams emerge from nothing — a single driver braking slightly creates a backwards-propagating wave of congestion.
Panic buying creates the shortages people feared: the prediction causes itself to come true.
Trust in institutions collapses non-linearly — it takes years to build and moments to destroy.
Housing bubbles form when the price signal (going up) is mistaken for fundamental value rather than collective speculation.
Diverse teams solve problems faster because they avoid correlated blind spots that homogeneous groups share.

# Knowledge & Learning
The best insights come from connecting ideas across different domains — analogies are bridges between separate knowledge islands.
Expertise is pattern recognition: chess masters don't think more moves ahead, they recognize more board patterns.
Spaced repetition works because each retrieval strengthens the memory trace more than passive review.
Understanding emerges from compression: you truly know something when you can explain it simply.
Creative breakthroughs often happen during rest, when the mind freely associates between loosely connected ideas.
The most important discoveries are often at the boundaries between fields, where different frameworks collide and recombine.
