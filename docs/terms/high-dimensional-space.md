# High-dimensional space

A space with thousands of directions, where random things almost never point the
same way.

## Like you're twelve

On a line, two random points are often close by accident. On a page there is
more room, so accidents get rarer. In a room, rarer still. Keep adding
directions and the space becomes so roomy that two things picked at random are
essentially never confusable — there is always another direction they differ in.

That is the whole trick. A big space is not a bigger filing cabinet; it is a
space where you can throw things in blindly and they still land apart.

## Precisely

In *d* dimensions, the angle between two randomly drawn vectors concentrates
around 90° as *d* grows, and the spread shrinks like 1/√d. By a few thousand
dimensions, near-[[Orthogonality|orthogonality]] is not a tendency but a
practical guarantee.

Two consequences follow, and every other idea in Heather leans on them.
Unrelated items do not collide, so distinct things stay distinct without anyone
assigning them ids. And [[Superposition|superposed]] vectors stay readable —
adding several vectors together does not smear them into an average, because
each term stays recoverable along its own direction.

Dimensionality is the resource here in the way memory is the resource elsewhere.
Spend more of it and you get more room to keep things apart.
